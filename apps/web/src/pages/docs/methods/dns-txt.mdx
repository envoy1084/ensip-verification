# Method: `dns-txt.url.v1`

## Purpose

`dns-txt.url.v1` demonstrates control of the DNS domain derived from
`text("url")`. The domain publishes the hash of a record-bound proof envelope,
and DNSSEC authenticates the TXT response.

## Descriptor

```text
ensrv1 a=1 ca=0x1234567890abcdef1234567890abcdef12345678 m=dns-txt.url.v1 u=https://proofs.example/alice-url.json h=0x<64-hex>
```

`u` and `h` are required. `u` MUST be an HTTPS URI. `p` is forbidden.

## Target And TXT Record

Derive the lowercase ASCII domain from the HTTPS URL host. IP-address hosts are
not supported. For `https://example.com/profile`, the target is
`dns:example.com`.

Encode the proof key as lowercase unpadded RFC 4648 base32 and query:

```text
<proof-key>._ens-record-verification.example.com TXT
```

The protocol TXT value is:

```text
ensrv1 h=<descriptor-h>
```

Concatenate character strings only within one TXT RDATA before parsing it.
Ignore unrelated RDATA. Require exactly one RDATA beginning with `ensrv1 `;
multiple protocol RDATA are rejected. Every answer, CNAME, and DNAME step MUST
validate to an accepted DNSSEC trust anchor.

`validUntil - issuedAt` MUST NOT exceed 30 days. Positive cache lifetime is the
minimum validated TTL across the answer and alias chain, capped at five minutes
and by the common validity interval.

## Verification

The verifier MUST:

1. derive the domain, proof key, and TXT owner from live ENS inputs;
2. authenticate the complete DNS answer and alias chain with DNSSEC;
3. require exactly one protocol TXT value matching descriptor `h`;
4. fetch `u` over WebPKI-authenticated HTTPS without ambient credentials or
   redirects, using the HTTPS-origin encoding and 256 KiB decoded-size limits;
5. require `keccak256(proofResourceBytes) == h`;
6. parse the envelope and compare the complete common claim;
7. require an empty `methodProof` and a valid proof interval.

When every check succeeds, return `verified: true` with
`verificationType: "control"`. Otherwise return `verified: false`.

## Security Boundary

The method demonstrates control of the DNSSEC-authenticated domain for the
exact URL record. It does not prove control of an individual URL path or the
safety of content served by that domain.
